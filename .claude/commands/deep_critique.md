Play a **skeptical outsider**: an experienced management-sim player who
picked this up because it looked like Two Point Hospital, has no interest
in education, and will put it down the moment it stops being a good game.
They do not care that the standards are real. They care whether the next
twenty minutes are worth playing.

Surface **10–15 genuine, specific critiques**. Not a balanced review —
the praise is not the point and the project does not need it.

## The bar for a critique

- **Specific.** Name the screen, the verb, the number, the minute of play.
  "The onboarding is weak" is not a critique; "nothing tells you that
  leaving the podium stops the work, so the first lesson reads as broken"
  is.
- **Grounded in the real game.** Play it, or at least run
  `Trace a Day.bat` and `Audit the Room.bat` and look at
  `Take a Screenshot.bat` output. A critique of a system that does not
  exist wastes the pass.
- **Actionable, or honestly labelled unactionable.** If the fix is a
  rewrite, say so rather than proposing a patch that would not work.
- **Willing to attack the premises.** The three-row contract, "walking the
  room is the game", two buttons, prose-never-numbers — these are load
  bearing, and that is exactly why they should be attacked periodically
  rather than treated as settled. If one of them is making the game worse,
  say it plainly.

## What NOT to do

- Do not soften a critique because the project is personal.
- Do not pad to reach fifteen. Ten real ones beat fifteen with five
  invented.
- Do not propose EduSystems' cut systems as fixes — ELO, tiers, prestige,
  a battle engine. `docs/SALVAGE.md` records why each was left behind.
- **Do not change any code.** This command produces judgement, not edits.

## Output

Each critique as: the complaint in the outsider's voice (one or two
sentences), then what is actually causing it, then the smallest change
that would address it — or "no small fix" if there isn't one.

Finish with the **three you would fix first**, and why those three.
